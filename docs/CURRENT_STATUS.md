## Repository reality

The repository implements the first playable version 0.1 race in Vite and Three.js. `index.html` is the start/results/HUD interface. `src/game.js` sets up the WebGL scene, camera follow, pointer/touch/keyboard steering and vertical throttle, one-lap race lifecycle, crest-triggered craft lift and airborne drift, elapsed-time result, and retry. The camera now follows 6.2 world units behind and 2.8 above the craft, aims 9 units ahead, and uses 48-degree desktop / 56-degree mobile framing with smooth follow; its visual appearance has not yet been interactively verified. `src/track.js` builds the closed circuit from the Neon Causeway definition in `src/track-data.js` (33 3D centerline control points, horizontal scale 3.25, width 10 world units); it creates the roadway, emissive edges, lane markers, instanced trackside posts/lights, and a width-matched start/finish gate. `src/craft.js` creates the player craft and engine accents. `src/style.css` provides responsive start/results presentation and HUD.

Automatic travel cruises at 65 world units/second (2,340 km/h); holding in the upper half of the touch/pointer surface smoothly raises speed by up to 30%, and holding in the lower half smoothly brakes by up to 30%. Releasing returns the target to cruise speed. Arrow-up/down or W/S provide matching keyboard input. The HUD reports current speed and thrust, brake, or air state. The craft gains a brief, gravity-settled lift when its sampled route tangent transitions sharply downward; lateral slide and steering response are looser while airborne. This is an arcade handling approximation, not a physical simulation. Race progress, finish, and lap timing continue to use elapsed time and actual travelled distance. The current sampled circuit remains approximately 2,967.5 units long (about 4.34 times the previous 683.96-unit layout) and has a smoothed elevation range of approximately -12 to +36 units (48 units total). The centerline has 33 control points and varies from straights and open turns to tighter S-bends and apex sections; a 1,400-point self-proximity check found no nonlocal points within the 10-unit road width (minimum sampled separation about 39.9 units). Road ribbons, lane markers, trackside supports/lights, the start/finish gate, track frames, and the player craft pose follow the 3D path. Repeated markers and scenery use instanced meshes. The chase camera follows 6.2 units behind and 2.8 units above the craft and looks 9 units ahead; its field of view is 48 degrees on desktop and 56 on mobile, with smoothly damped position and aim. These are implementation settings, not visually playtested camera tuning. At 65 units/second, length divided by speed implies a lap time of about 45.7 seconds; this is an estimate, not an observed race result.

Analytic crest and control checks plus build/syntax/whitespace verification passed; the Vite development server also started and reported ready at `http://127.0.0.1:5173/`. At a sampled constant 65 units/s, the current curve triggered three lift events, with peak craft lift of approximately 0.7 world units; configured speed limits are 45.5 to 84.5 units/s (±30%). These checks exercise numerical route samples, configured controls and camera settings, not actual pointer/touch events, race behavior, or camera composition. ## Product direction

The intended product is a fast-paced mobile 3D anti-gravity racer inspired by Wipeout 2097, built with Three.js and original content. See `GDD.md` for staged scope. Version 0.1 is a one-track, one-craft single-player race. CPU rivals, pause/resume, richer presentation, music and effects, multiple tracks/craft, and launch-scale polish are not implemented. Product goals must not be described as implemented features.

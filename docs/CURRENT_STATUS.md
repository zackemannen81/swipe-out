# Current Status

Last verified: 2026-10-10

## Repository reality

The repository implements the first competitive version 0.1 race in Vite and Three.js. `index.html` is the start/results/HUD interface. `src/game.js` sets up the WebGL scene, camera follow, input, race lifecycle, HUD updates, one-lap finish/results/retry, crest-triggered craft lift, airborne drift, player placement, race audio integration, and audio-reactive player thrusters. `src/audio.js` plays `public/audio/tracks/track_002.mp3` after the start gesture and exposes smoothed kick, bass, mid, high, and hi-hat analyser channels extracted from the visualizer prototype; kick/bass modulate the engine glow and paired underbody thruster plumes. `src/opponents.js` creates two visually distinct procedural CPU craft, advances them along fixed lane neighborhoods at deterministic speeds, records their finish times, and resets them for Race Again. `src/craft.js` creates the procedural craft, engine accents, glow, and paired rear thruster geometry; rival thrusters remain hidden outside the player race loop. The camera follows 6.2 world units behind and 2.8 above the craft, aims 9 units ahead, and uses 48-degree desktop / 56-degree mobile framing with smooth follow. `src/track.js` builds the closed circuit from the Neon Causeway definition in `src/track-data.js` (33 3D centerline control points, horizontal scale 3.25, width 10 world units); it creates the roadway, emissive edges, lane markers, instanced trackside posts/lights, and a width-matched start/finish gate. `src/craft.js` creates the player and rival craft plus engine accents. `src/style.css` provides responsive start/results presentation and HUD.

Automatic travel cruises at 65 world units/second (2,340 km/h); holding in the upper half of the touch/pointer surface smoothly raises speed by up to 30%, and holding in the lower half smoothly brakes by up to 30%. Releasing returns the target to cruise speed. Arrow-up/down or W/S provide matching keyboard input. The HUD reports current speed, thrust, brake, or air state and live position in a three-racer field. Vanta cruises at 60 units/second and Kestrel at 70.5 units/second; both use subtle deterministic lateral variation, with no collision system, rubber-banding, or advanced track-aware strategy. The craft gains a brief, gravity-settled lift when its sampled route tangent transitions sharply toward a descent; lateral slide and steering response are looser while airborne. Race distance, finish, placement, and retry use the one-lap race loop.

The current sampled circuit remains approximately 2,967.5 units long (about 4.34 times the previous 683.96-unit layout) and has a smoothed elevation range of approximately -12 to +36 units (48 units total). The centerline has 33 control points and varies from straights and open turns to tighter S-bends and apex sections; a 1,400-point self-proximity check found no nonlocal points within the 10-unit road width (minimum sampled separation about 39.9 units). Road ribbons, lane markers, trackside supports/lights, the start/finish gate, track frames, and craft poses follow the 3D path. Repeated markers and scenery use instanced meshes. The chase camera follows 6.2 units behind and 2.8 units above the craft and looks 9 units ahead; its field of view is 48 degrees on desktop and 56 on mobile, with smoothly damped position and aim. These are implementation settings, not visually playtested camera tuning. At 65 units/second, length divided by speed implies a lap time of about 45.7 seconds; this is an estimate, not an observed race result.

Analytic crest/control checks, CPU distance/position/reset simulation, production build, JavaScript syntax checks, and whitespace verification passed; the Vite development server previously started and reported ready at `http://127.0.0.1:5173/`. These checks exercise numerical route samples, configured controls, rival movement/reset, and camera settings, not actual pointer/touch events, race behavior, visual composition, or physical-device behavior. Browser automation could not start a session, so vertical touch mapping, crest-jump feel, airborne slide, finish/results/retry, rival visibility, final placement, visual appearance including the closer camera, and physical-device behavior have not been interactively verified.

This pass has no automated regression-test suite; `npm run build` does not test race behavior. Report actual interaction checks separately instead of using build success as evidence that start, steering, throttle/braking, crest jumps, finish, results, retry, or visual tuning works in the browser.

## Product direction

The intended product is a fast-paced mobile 3D anti-gravity racer inspired by Wipeout 2097, built with Three.js and original content. See `GDD.md` for staged scope. Version 0.1 now has a one-track, three-racer single-player competition prototype. Audio, pause/resume, richer presentation, multiple tracks/craft, and launch-scale polish are not implemented. Product goals must not be described as implemented features.
